The two lines really do move in opposite directions. When there is loads of time the tip is worth
between nothing and about 5 per cent, and when time is short it is worth up to 53 per cent but you
could only afford to pay about a quarter of what an ordinary answer costs. The race is the plainest
version: with no impossible questions at all in the pile, the checker still spent time checking, and
finished fewer.

Nothing here was faked. Each time it was an honest count that nobody had checked: five reasons for
dropping one idea turned out to lean on two, four write-ups that agreed turned out to have inherited
a single word from the same parent essay, and two questions that looked different turned out to be
the same question asked twice. Somebody had to sit down and count. That, and the write-ups that
argued themselves out of existence and said so, is the part worth copying.
