# 0. The verdict, first

**"Stories are a force" does not survive as physics, and the sharpest reason is not
uncomputability. It is that a good story is not a short story.** The best available
formalisation identifies narrative causality with a simplicity prior over histories,
`P(h) ∝ 2^{-K(h)}`, which predicts that the *boring* history is the likely one. Narrativium's
own slogan says the opposite: a million-to-one chance coming off is a **high-surprisal** event,
and surprisal is exactly what a code charges for. The identification fails at first contact
with the phenomenon it was built for, and no machinery around it repairs that sign.

**What survives is a demotion, and the owner's own text already states it.** His chosen quote
(`essays/Narrativium.md` l.18, emphasis added) reads: *"Humans **add** narrativium to their
world. They insist on interpreting the universe as if it's telling a story. This leads them to
focus on facts that fit the story, while ignoring those that don't."* That is a claim about
observers and their compression habits, not about dynamics. Narrativium's formal content is a
theorem about bounded describers, it belongs beside [`omniscience.md`](omniscience.md), and it
points the **opposite way to the myth**: a story is available for only an exponentially small
fraction of histories.

**The honest physics analogue is maximum caliber**, which is the owner's own method: the
`physics/entropy.md` maximum-entropy calculation lifted from states to trajectories. It is
inference bookkeeping, not a force, and §3 marks exactly where that line falls.

# 1. The strongest formalisation, built properly

## 1.1 The construction

Fix an encoding of histories as finite binary strings and a universal prefix machine `U`. Let
`K(h)` be the prefix Kolmogorov complexity of history `h`. The **algorithmic prior** is

$$ P(h) \;=\; \frac{2^{-K(h)}}{Z}, \qquad Z \;=\; \sum_h 2^{-K(h)} \;\le\; 1 . $$

The bound `Z ≤ 1` is Kraft's inequality on the prefix-free set of shortest programs, so this is
a genuine sub-probability measure rather than a formal expression. The construction is well
posed, which is why it deserves attacking properly rather than waving away. In Gibbs form:

$$ P(h) \;=\; \frac{e^{-\beta E(h)}}{Z}, \qquad E(h) = K(h), \quad \beta = \ln 2 . $$

The algorithmic prior is a Boltzmann distribution whose energy is description length and whose
temperature is fixed at `1/\ln 2`. `physics/entropy.md` maximises `-\sum p_k \ln p_k` under
constraints and reads off exactly this shape; [`information-wing.md`](information-wing.md)
argues that the wing's single quantity is `\log W`, and `K` is `\log` of a count of programs.
This is the same object again in a third coat, which is a point in the construction's favour
and, later, a point against calling it a *new* idea.

## 1.2 What dynamics it would induce

Speculation, labelled: taking the prior seriously as a *mechanism* means the world samples a
whole history from `P`, so the observed history minimises `K` subject to whatever is held
fixed. Two structural consequences follow immediately, and both are fatal in §2.4:

- the weight is assigned to a **complete history**, past and future together, so this is a
  boundary condition on the whole of spacetime, not a rule for what happens next;
- `K` is not additive over subsystems (`K(x,y) ≤ K(x) + K(y) + O(\log)` and the gap is real),
  so the measure does not factorise and there is no local Lagrangian density that generates it.

# 2. Where it breaks, and it breaks in six places

## 2.1 A good story is not a short story (the fatal one)

This objection kills the identification, so it goes first rather than last. Narrative
satisfaction is not description length. The Discworld gag is that the **million-to-one** chance
succeeds; in any code matched to the event statistics a probability-`p` outcome costs
`-\log_2 p` bits, and that is monotone the wrong way:

$$ p < q \;\Longrightarrow\; -\log q \;<\; -\log p \veq{nsurp}\lean $$

so the dramatic coincidence *lengthens* the history's description. Quantitatively, and with no
logarithm needed, `2^{19} < 10^6 < 2^{20}`: a million-to-one chance costs between nineteen and
twenty bits to record. The simplicity prior suppresses it by a factor near `10^{-6}`; the myth
says it happens nine times out of ten. The two are not merely different, they are opposed.

**Verdict on the identification: dead.** Anything that rescues it must explain why the class of
"good stories" is simultaneously improbable-in-the-event and short-in-description.

## 2.2 The two-part-code rescue, examined and largely rejected

The obvious rescue is MDL's two-part code, which is genuinely the right shape: a story is a
short **model** plus a few dramatic **exceptions**, and total cost is

$$ L(h) \;=\; L(\text{model}) + L(h \mid \text{model}) \veq{ntwo}\lean $$

The rescue captures something true. A narrative *is* a compressive model with a residual, and
the striking event lives in the residual, where its high surprisal is expected rather than
anomalous. But it does not save the identification, for two reasons.

**First, the exceptions are charged at full price.** The Lean file proves the optimum is
attained at an actual model whose length is part of the bill (`bestTwoPart_pays_a_model`), and
that a two-part code cannot beat a given one-part code by more than the null model's length
(`twopart_rescue_bounded`). Both are bookkeeping, and saying so *is* the finding: there is no
free narrative frame, and the twenty bits still get paid.

**Second, unrestricted it explains everything and therefore nothing.** Any history at all is "a
short model plus exceptions" if the exception list may be arbitrarily long. The rescue acquires
content only when the model class is fixed in advance and the residual capped. A fixed model
class with a residual budget has a name in the humanities: a **genre**. That is a real object,
but it is a property of *tellers*, not of worlds, which is §5's demotion arriving early.

## 2.3 Uncomputability, which is the weakest of the objections

`K` is uncomputable, so `P(h) ∝ 2^{-K(h)}` cannot be evaluated by anything inside the universe
it governs. The counting machinery is in [`information-wing.md`](information-wing.md) (§1,
`exists_incompressible`, `few_compressible`) and the same diagonal in its logical coat is in
[`omniscience.md`](omniscience.md): a subsystem cannot hold a complete description of a whole
containing it, by **arity, not capacity**.

It is listed third because a determined speculator can dodge it: a *measure* need not be
computed by any agent to be the right measure. The version that does not dodge is §2.1.

A second, sharper version does bite: the invariance theorem fixes `K` only up to an additive
machine-dependent constant, `|K_U(h) - K_V(h)| ≤ c_{UV}`. Over an infinite family that constant
is harmless. Over the finite set of candidate histories any concrete comparison uses, the
constant can exceed every difference being compared, and the "preferred" history becomes a
choice of programming language. The prior has a gauge freedom and no gauge fixing.

## 2.4 It is a boundary condition, not a force

Even granting a computable surrogate for `K`, the object built in §1.2 weights a whole history
including its future, so it is not a force, not a field equation, and not Markovian. The correct
comparison is not Newton's second law but the **Past Hypothesis**: a constraint on the boundary
of history space. Physics entertains such objects and they are respectable, but they are
categorically not the kind of thing that pushes anything around a stage. Calling a boundary
condition a force is the error, and it is the same error twice given the non-factorisation of
§1.2.

## 2.5 Anthropic contamination: selection on observation, not on dynamics

Two claims must be kept apart, and conflating them is the whole of the folk appeal:

| | Claim | Where it lives | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D** | The world's history measure is biased toward story-shaped histories | dynamics | no evidence, and §2.1 says the bias would run the wrong way |
| **O** | The histories we *notice, retain and retell* are biased toward story-shaped ones | observation | overwhelmingly supported, and it is the owner's own quote |

**O** requires no new physics. Story-shaped events are memorable, transmissible and cheap to
encode once the genre is shared, so any bounded, gossiping recorder over-retains them, and that
alone produces the entire observed phenomenon: the felt frequency of million-to-one chances is a
property of the archive, not of the sample space. Concluding **D** from it is a selection effect
read as a mechanism.

## 2.6 Falsifiability: statable, and it has effectively been run

The honest answer is not "unfalsifiable". Post hoc it is untestable, because the "good story"
predicate is applied after the outcome is known and fits anything. Pre-registered it is a real
test: fix a corpus of well-measured stochastic events with a known null (lottery draws, decay
timings), have blinded raters rank the *possible* outcomes for narrative satisfaction **before**
they are revealed, then test for excess frequency of top-ranked outcomes. That is a standard
`\chi^2` on a pre-registered partition, and nothing prevents running it. Its result is also
already known in every practical sense: the working practice of statistics, gambling mathematics
and detector calibration is a continuous large-`n` test of this null and no excess has ever been
reported. **Claim D is not unfalsifiable. It is falsifiable and, to the precision that matters,
falsified.**

# 3. Where a real version of this idea already lives in physics

Four candidates weight histories, and sorting "physical principle" from "inference bookkeeping"
is this section's useful content.

| Formalism | Weight on a history | Physical principle, or bookkeeping? |
|---|---|---|
| Path integral | `\exp(iS/\hbar)` | **Physical.** The amplitude, not a probability; interference is observed. |
| Least action | stationarity of `S` | **Physical**, and it is a *theorem about* the path integral, not a separate postulate. |
| Maximum caliber | `\exp(-\sum_k \lambda_k A_k)` over trajectories | **Bookkeeping**, and honest about it: Jaynes-style inference given trajectory constraints. |
| Algorithmic prior | `2^{-K(h)}` | **Bookkeeping**, and uncomputable, and gauge-dependent (§2.3). |

**Least action already looks teleological and already is not.** Feynman's gloss is the right
one: the particle does not survey all paths and choose, the stationary path dominates because
neighbouring paths interfere constructively there and nowhere else. So physics *does* have a
preferred-history principle, it is **extremal**, and it is derived rather than posited. In the
only case that is finite algebra the statement is exact. For `S(x) = a x^2 + b x + c`, `a ≠ 0`,

$$ S'(x) = 0 \iff x = -\frac{b}{2a} \veq{nstat}\lean $$

and the stationary path is the minimum exactly when the sign condition holds:

$$ \bigl(\forall x,\; S(x_\star) \le S(x)\bigr) \iff a > 0 \veq{nmin}\lean $$

The `\iff` in the second is the part worth keeping: extremal is not minimal, so even the real
preferred-history principle needs a sign condition before "the universe prefers this" means
anything. For `a < 0` the same unique stationary path is the *worst* history in the family.

**The path integral and the owner's entropy chapter are already one formalism.** The sibling
[`wick-entropy.md`](wick-entropy.md) shows `\exp(iS/\hbar)` and `\exp(-\beta E)` are the same
weight Wick-rotated, so toesnail's "preferred history" and "preferred state" languages are one
idea, with `physics/entropy.md` already on the Euclidean side of it.

**Maximum caliber is the strongest honest answer to the seed.** It is literally
`physics/entropy.md`'s Lagrange-multiplier calculation with the index running over trajectories
instead of states, and it yields the sentence the seed was reaching for: *given what you know
about a process, these histories are the ones to bet on.* Its status is inference, not
mechanism. The maximum-entropy theorem it rests on is `entropy_le_log_card` in
[`lean/InfoWing.lean`](lean/InfoWing.lean), not reproved here: with the index read as
trajectories it is character-for-character the same proof, and a copy would be padding.

# 4. Narrativium's actual mathematical content, and it inverts the myth

Strip the metaphysics and one theorem is left. Let histories be `n`-bit strings and let a
"story" be any description shorter than `n` bits, decoded by an arbitrary `dec` with no
computability assumption. Splitting `n = m + c`:

$$ \frac{\#\{\text{histories with a story of} < m \text{ bits}\}}{2^{n}} \;\le\; 2^{-c} \veq{nrare}\lean $$

Ten bits of narrative economy restricts you to one history in a thousand; twenty bits, to about
one in a million. **A short account exists for an exponentially small fraction of histories**,
so the world is overwhelmingly made of things that cannot be told shorter than they happened.

That is the exact inverse of the myth, and it implies the observer-side claim of §2.5 directly:
a bounded describer who insists on stories must be discarding almost everything, which is the
second sentence of the owner's own Pratchett quote with a number attached.

# 5. The verdict, and the demotion said plainly

**Narrativium is formalisable, but not as anything in the world.** Precisely:

1. **As a force or a history measure: no.** The identification with a simplicity prior is the
   best available and it fails on sign (§2.1), on locality and temporal structure (§2.4), and
   on gauge (§2.3). Its rescue via two-part codes buys a bounded amount and, unrestricted,
   explains everything (§2.2). Claim D is testable and unsupported (§2.6).
2. **As a theorem about bounded describers: yes, and it is sharp.** Agents with bounded
   description length must compress histories into stories; the compression is lossy; and the
   loss is characterisable, at rate `2^{-c}` (§4), with the residual charged in full (§2.2).
3. **The demotion is Pratchett's own.** "Humans *add* narrativium to their world" is already a
   claim about humans. The essay wing has been carrying the correct version of the idea since
   the day it was quoted; only the pun made it look like physics.

This lands squarely next to [`omniscience.md`](omniscience.md): both say that the interesting
limit is on the *describer*, not on the described, and neither needs any physics to say it.
