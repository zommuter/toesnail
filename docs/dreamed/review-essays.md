## Findings, by severity

### HIGH

**1. The "clinching" broadcasting refutation of the ball is invalid, and it is recorded as the
driver of a ratified ruling.**
File: `logic-complementarity.md` §6.3 and §8 (ranked "hardest to overturn"); propagated verbatim
into `fig-the-object.md` ("The clinching argument needs no geometry"), the `fig-cluster-map.md`
D1 row ("The finding behind it"), and `docs/meeting-notes/2026-09-07-1508-bloch-truth-rulings.md`
D1 ("The clinching argument needs no geometry. A non-simplicial state space is exactly one with
no broadcasting ... so a ball would assert that a sentence's status cannot be copied. Publishing
a proof copies it, exactly and freely.").

What is wrong: the cited theorem does not say that. BBLW Theorem 2 says *universal* broadcasting
(every state) is possible only for a simplex. BBLW Theorem 3, verbatim: *"A set of states is
broadcastable iff it lies in a simplex generated by jointly distinguishable states."* The two
poles of a qubit are jointly distinguishable, so **the poles are broadcastable in quantum theory
too**. "Publishing a proof copies a status" copies a pole. That is consistent with a ball, a
simplex, and every theory in between. To refute the ball by broadcasting one would have to show
that *every* state, including the equatorial and off-axis ones, can be copied, which requires
knowing what those states mean, which is exactly the open question. The essay's claim that the
argument "does not need any assumption about what Omega is" is therefore false: it silently
assumes the status of a sentence is always a pole-like classical datum, and then finds it
classical. The essay's own §6.4 concedes the general form of this gap and then exempts §6.3 from
it. A quantum-information reader will spot this in one line, and it sits on a public page
attributed to a named PRL.

Fix: strike the "clinching argument" paragraph from `logic-complementarity.md` §6.3, §8 item 1
and Surfaced item 3, and from both figure pages; replace with "the two poles are jointly
distinguishable, hence broadcastable by BBLW Thm 3, which every candidate geometry satisfies;
broadcasting cannot decide between them." D1 itself does not fall: it still rests on the four
azimuth essays. But the meeting note records the broken argument as clinching, and only the owner
can amend a ratified note. Recommend he does, so the record does not carry a refuted proof.

**2. The Weltformel essay proposes reporting a "located error" to living authors that has a
plain charitable reading.**
File: `weltformel-impossibility.md` §1.1 and §8 item 3: *"A universe cannot be inconsistent.
Consistency is a property of sets of sentences, not of worlds ... That category error is what
carries P1's escape"*, with the ruling "whether this is reported to the authors."

What is wrong: P1's sentence ("it is impossible for actual S_ToE in the Platonic realm to be
inconsistent, as this would produce real inconsistencies in the universe") reads naturally as
the soundness theorem: a theory that is true of the world has the world as a model, hence is
consistent. That is valid, not a category error. The genuine objection is different and the
essay already has it elsewhere: Lucas-Penrose needs the *reasoner's* system to be consistent and
to *know* it, and "the true S_ToE is consistent" says nothing about either. Item 3 as written
would embarrass the project if sent. Item 2 (the Chaitin misstatement, §3.3) is correct and
stands; the `r = r` counterexample is sound.

Fix: rewrite §1.1's last paragraph to "P1's argument is valid for the *true* theory but Lucas-
Penrose needs consistency of the *mathematician's* theory, which is not the same object; the
equivocation is between the two", and demote §8 item 3 from "located error" to "rhetorical
shortcut, not reportable". Keep item 2.

### MEDIUM

**3. "Provable / not provable" is not a two-outcome procedure, and that claim is the surviving
argument for encoding (b).**
Files: `logic-bloch-poles.md` §1(b) (*"the only one that corresponds to an actual two-outcome
procedure: run the proof search, ask whether it halts with a proof"*), repeated at §9 item 1;
`logic-models-vs-epistemic.md` §4 (*"a two-outcome procedure a machine can actually run"*);
`fig-the-object.md:55`; README row for `logic-bloch-poles`.

What is wrong: provability in an r.e. theory is semi-decidable. The procedure returns "yes" or
runs forever; it never returns "not provable". The dichotomy is metatheoretic, not operational,
which is the opposite of what the sentence claims. The adjudicator explicitly leaves encoding (b)
standing *on this argument* after dissolving the pigeonhole, so (b) currently rests on a false
sentence. Fix: replace "two-outcome procedure" with "a genuine dichotomy in the classical
metatheory whose second outcome no procedure can return"; the adjudicator's §4 qualification
then needs re-weighing.

**4. `logic-thermodynamics` dresses a tautology as a correction of four siblings.**
File: §4.3 and §12 item 2; README row (*"The quantity that sharpens at the SAT threshold is the
truth lean z, which corrects four sibling essays"*).

What is wrong: the satisfiability threshold is *defined* as the density where P(SAT) crosses
1/2, and z = 2P(SAT) minus 1. That z is the order parameter of the SAT transition is the
definition of the SAT transition. The siblings' "r is the informative axis" is a claim about
epistemic settledness, not about random 3-SAT, and the essay does not show they are in tension.
The rest of the essay (Hagedorn structure, Landauer margin, "T_c knows nothing about the goal")
is well calibrated. Fix: §4.3 should say "in random k-SAT the transition is by construction in
z; at fixed budget r is a completion fraction"; delete "corrects four sibling essays" from the
README row and item 2.

**5. `wirohsh-splats` §2(c) is false as written, has been corrected by a sibling, and carries no
pointer.**
File: `wirohsh-splats.md:151-157` (*"no annulus contains it ... global only in the degenerate

**6. `model-attestation` reads on a public page as an accusation against commercial providers.**

essay's own §1 says the *attack that pays* is silent substitution, and then lets the 11-of-31
figure stand in for evidence of it. Fix: "differ from the reference distribution, mostly by

Also: DiFR (arXiv:2511.20621), "Chain & Hash (ICLR 2026)" and the 61-72% Kimi K2 figure were

### LOW

**8. "NO-GO at theorem strength" (`five-level-laser` §8) is a theorem about a stated topology,
and the topology assumption is not in the verdict sentence.** I re-derived the chain bound: in
steady state the single-cycle flux J is common to all links, J > 0 yields every per-link
inequality the Lean takes as hypothesis, and J <= 0 gives N3 <= N4 from the laser link alone.
So the prose argument is sound. But it assumes every route into level 3 passes through the lift
2 -> 3; a parasitic channel that populates |3> directly from |1> or |0> (excited-state
absorption of a pump photon lands near 1.8 eV, per the essay's own §6.3) breaks the chain, and
the Monte Carlo covered only bypass *decays* (A30, A20), not bypass *pumping*. Fix: add "within
the single-cycle topology; direct pumping into |3> is outside the theorem" to §8.

## Verdict

Fit to be public as it stands, with the standing UNREVIEWED banner doing real work, on two
conditions. Item 1 must be fixed before anyone is pointed at the Bloch Truth cluster: it is a
refuted argument, propagated to three pages and into the ratified record, contradicted by the
verbatim statement of the theorem it cites, and it is the sort of thing a competent reader
finds in the first minute. Item 2 must be fixed before any contact with the Faizal authors is
contemplated. Everything else is the ordinary residue of a large speculative batch: over-firm
verbs in a handful of headlines, one tautology sold as a correction, one uncorrected retraction,
and a citation audit that stopped two hours before the last four essays. The maths that was
checked here reproduced; the physics essays are careful; the Lean files prove small true things
and say so. Findings: 2 HIGH, 5 MEDIUM, 4 LOW.
