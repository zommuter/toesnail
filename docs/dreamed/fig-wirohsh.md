# The anatomy of a NO-GO

Five essays of the 2026-09-01 dreaming session were seeded on the owner's unfinished
`physics/wirohsh.md` and on ROADMAP item `id:ff32`. Between them they recommend closing `id:ff32`.
**That recommendation is UNRATIFIED exploration. It is a recommendation awaiting the owner's
ruling, not a decision**, and `TODO.md` records `id:ff32` as open with a neutral pointer, exactly
so that no verdict is recorded on his behalf.

This page draws the cluster because a plain list would hide its one interesting structural
question: the index summarises the cluster as five routes converging on one NO-GO, and a
convergence of five is a much stronger thing than a convergence of one. A sibling essay in another
cluster ([`logic-models-vs-epistemic`](logic-models-vs-epistemic.md)) deflated a four-way
convergence to *"one theorem applied four times, not four theorems"*. The same audit is owed here,
and it is §3 below. Its answer, stated up front so nothing is teased: **the convergence is real but
it is two-fold, not five-fold**, and only two of the five essays bear on `id:ff32` at all.

## 1. The claim under test

`ROADMAP.md id:ff32` (INBOUND `routed:b0c5` from `loderite`) asks one question:

> can a precomputed per-geometry response basis (T-matrix analogy) represent Gaussian splats
> compactly via WiRoHSH?

Three objects, joined pairwise, and the whole item lives or dies on both joins holding.

Separately, `physics/wirohsh.md` dies mid-formula: it reaches `f(x,y,z) = \int_0^{2\pi} d\phi\ (\ )`
and stops with the parentheses empty. [`wirohsh-splats.md`](wirohsh-splats.md) §1 fills them, and
that half is a **success**, not a failure: the reduction closes because the Wick rotation makes the
propagation direction null, and the completed formula is Whittaker's 1903 representation of
harmonic functions, character for character. The owner re-derived a 123-year-old theorem, correctly,
by his own route. Keep those two things apart while reading the rest of this page. The formula
closing is not a reason for the NO-GO; it is the thing the NO-GO is about the *use* of.

Two further details the audit turns up, both of which cut against the cluster rather than for it.

**Break 3 rests on a claim its own sibling later corrected.** `wirohsh-splats.md` §2(c) wrote *"no
annulus contains the real line"*; `wirohsh-approximation.md` §3.3 calls that *"too strong"*, since
the degenerate annulus is the whole plane and an entire profile is globally represented. Break 3
survives the correction (a splat cloud is not entire), but it is weaker than round 1 stated it.

**The ladder essay's locality bullet is inherited, not derived.** `wirohsh-ladder.md` §5 argues a
rung makes locality worse, and cites `wirohsh-approximation.md` for the premise. That is a citation
edge, and counting it as a sixth arrival would be exactly the *"shared inheritance of one word from
the parent"* error the Bloch cluster's adjudicator named.

What the audit does **not** do is weaken the recommendation. Two independent roots, each sufficient
on its own, is a sound basis for a NO-GO. It weakens only the *rhetoric* of five-fold convergence,
and the value of saying so is that a five-fold convergence would have been much harder for the owner
to reopen than a two-fold one, on grounds he may yet find.

The reason is the identity theorem, and it is one line: a nonzero function holomorphic on a
connected open set containing an interval on which it vanishes is identically zero, so no nonzero
holomorphic function has compact support. Meanwhile `1/(1+x^2)` is merely smooth on the reals and
sits on the *other* side, because it extends to the strip below its poles at plus and minus i.

`wirohsh-discontinuities.md` §7.1 also measures the replacement criterion rather than asserting it.
The regularity of a profile is read off the growth of its Laurent coefficients on the shell, and
the criterion is

$$ \limsup_{|m|\to\infty}\big|f_m\big|^{1/|m|} = 1 \iff \text{the profile is nowhere the restriction of a locally holomorphic function} $$
