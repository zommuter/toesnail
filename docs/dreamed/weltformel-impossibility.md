# 0. The headline, stated and not teased

**"A world formula is impossible" is false as stated, and the papers do not prove it.** What they
establish, conditional on an assumption asserted in one sentence and never checked, is the 1933
result that a formal system cannot define its own truth predicate. That forbids
**self-validation**, not **physics**. Their rescue, an "external truth predicate" adjoined to
build a "Meta-Theory of Everything", is Tarski's own metalanguage from the same 1933 paper,
renamed. In Lean it costs one line and no Platonic realm:

$$\exists\, \mathrm{Tr} : S \to \mathrm{Prop},\ \forall s,\ \mathrm{Tr}(s) \leftrightarrow \mathrm{eval}(s) \veq{ext}\lean$$

Three claims travel under the impossibility banner and get three different verdicts (§6). The
papers' best moment, which their critics undersell: the **Tarski step is not decoration on the
Goedel step**, because it survives dropping the effective axiomatizability Goedel I requires
(§2.1). Their worst: a misstatement of Chaitin's theorem that is false as written, with a
one-line counterexample (§3.3).

# 1. What the papers claim, and where the weight sits

**P1 (2024)** proposes ten axioms for a Theory of Everything $S_{\text{ToE}}$, argues Tarski
forbids an internal truth predicate for it, then invokes Lucas-Penrose to conclude that
"non-algorithmic understanding" exists in a Platonic realm. **P2 (2025)** models quantum gravity
as $F_{QG} = \{L_{QG}, \Sigma_{QG}, R_{\text{alg}}\}$, applies Goedel I, Goedel II, Tarski and
Chaitin, then adjoins an external truth predicate $T$ and a non-effective rule:

$$M_{\text{ToE}} = \{L_{QG} \cup \{T\},\ \Sigma_{QG} \cup \Sigma_T,\ R_{\text{alg}} \cup R_{\text{nonalg}}\}$$

with condition (S4): the induced theory $\mathrm{Th}_T$ is not recursively enumerable. Because a
simulation would be algorithmic and omit the $T$-truths, P2 concludes the universe is not one.

Neither paper contains a theorem whose statement mentions a physical quantity. Every theorem cited
is a 1931-to-1974 result of logic, quoted, not proved, not extended. Three premises carry
everything, and none is defended:

1. **A**, physics is a formal system with an effective proof relation: asserted (P2's abstract,
   *"Quantum gravity is therefore envisaged as an axiomatic structure"*). This is a substantive
   position, that the laws of nature *are* a deductive theory rather than being *described by*
   one, and it is what Redden attacks from the other side (§4).
2. **B**, that system interprets enough arithmetic: asserted. P1 p. 5, in full: *"Here, the formal
   system $S_{\text{ToE}}$ is definitely robust enough to cover the arithmetic."* "Definitely" is
   the argument.
3. **C**, that system is consistent: asserted via an equivocation (§1.1).

## 1.1 What "consistent" quantifies over

The papers answer this three ways in three places:

| Where | "Consistent" means |
|---|---|
| P1 Axiom 1 | *"Thus, where applicable, it is renormalizable"* |
| P2 criteria | *"Strings secure this via anomaly cancellation; LQG via an anomaly-free constraint algebra"* |
| P1 p. 7, P2 §Goedel | $\Sigma \nvdash \bot$ (deductive) |

Only the third is the hypothesis Goedel and Tarski take. Renormalizability is power counting on a
Lagrangian; anomaly cancellation is gauge current conservation at one loop. Neither is a proof
that a deductive closure omits $\bot$, and by Goedel II no such proof is available from inside
anyway, which P2 says three sentences later. A reader who accepts P1's Axiom 1 because string
theory is anomaly-free has accepted nothing Goedel II needs.

P1 then makes the equivocation load-bearing. The standard objection to Lucas-Penrose is that a
human mathematician might be an *inconsistent* system, in which case "seeing" a Goedel sentence's
truth proves nothing. P1's answer (p. 7): *"these criticisms do not apply to the actual
$S_{\text{ToE}}$ in the Platonic realm, as it is impossible for actual $S_{\text{ToE}}$ in the
Platonic realm to be inconsistent, as this would produce real inconsistencies in the
universe/multiverse."*

**A universe cannot be inconsistent.** Consistency is a property of sets of sentences, not of
worlds. There are only descriptions that contradict each other, and the world is not a
description. That category error is what carries P1's escape from the one criticism everybody
makes.

## 1.2 Two further located defects in P1

- **Eq. (0.6)**: $\forall \varphi \in S_{\text{ToE}},\ S_{\text{ToE}} \vdash
  T(\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner) \leftrightarrow \mathrm{True}(\varphi)$ mixes object-language
  provability with a metalanguage `True` on the right, which is the very object whose
  non-existence is at stake. Tarski's statement uses the sentence itself:
  $T(\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner) \leftrightarrow \varphi$.
- **Eq. (0.7)** sets $G(x) \equiv \neg T(x)$ then substitutes $x = \ulcorner G\urcorner$. As
  written $G$ *is* the formula $\neg T$, so $\ulcorner G \urcorner$ codes a formula with a free
  variable, not a sentence. The real proof needs the **diagonal lemma** to produce $L$ with
  $L \leftrightarrow \neg T(\ulcorner L \urcorner)$, and that lemma is exactly what requires
  arithmetic. The paper skips the step its own contested Premise B was needed for.

## 1.3 The located defect that carries P2's physics

P2 p. 4 defines $\mathrm{True}(F_{QG}) = \{\varphi \in L_{QG} \mid \mathbb{N} \models \varphi\}$.
**Semantic truth for a quantum-gravity language is defined as satisfaction in the standard model
of arithmetic.** $\mathbb{N}$ has one sort and successor, addition, multiplication. It does not
interpret "quantum states, fields, curvature, causal relations", which is P2's own gloss on
$L_{QG}$'s symbols three paragraphs earlier. So the strict containment
$\mathrm{Th}(F_{QG}) \subsetneq \mathrm{True}(F_{QG})$ that P2 gets from Goedel I is a fact about
the *arithmetic fragment*. The next sentence reads *"Physically these Goedel sentences correspond
to empirically meaningful facts, e.g. specific black-hole microstates"*, with no argument. That
unsupported clause is the entire physical payload of the paper.

# 2. Is the Tarski step doing work Goedel could not?

## 2.1 Yes, and this is the papers' best moment

Goedel I needs the axiom set to be **effectively axiomatizable** (recursive, or at least r.e.).
Tarski's undefinability theorem does not. Tarski applies to **true arithmetic**
$\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{N})$, a complete consistent theory that is not r.e. and to which Goedel I
does not apply at all.

This matters concretely here. Answer the Goedel objection with "the laws of nature are not a
finite axiom list, they are the set of all physical truths" and you defeat Goedel I by falsifying
its effectivity hypothesis. You do **not** defeat Tarski. Building on Tarski is the correct
technical choice for the argument being attempted. Whether the authors made it for that reason is
not something this essay can know.

The Lean companion displays exactly this. `no_internal_truth_predicate` takes sentences, a
valuation into `Prop`, a negation matched to it, and a diagonal, and takes **no consistency and no
effectivity hypothesis**:

$$\neg\,\exists\, T : S \to S,\ \forall s,\ \mathrm{eval}(T(s)) \leftrightarrow \mathrm{eval}(s) \veq{tarski}\lean$$

`no_internal_truth_predicate_of_complete` adds semantic completeness as a deliberately redundant
hypothesis, to show completeness is not what Tarski takes away.

## 2.2 But the hypothesis it does need was never checked

Tarski needs a language interpreting enough arithmetic to support the diagonal lemma, plus
negation. The Lean file makes this visible instead of hiding it: `Diagonal` is a `structure`
field, an assumption you must supply, not a theorem. That is the honest encoding of P1 p. 5.

The threshold is real, and the owner has already asked the right question about it. His own chat
*Euclidean Axioms and Set Theory* (2025-08-08):

> "Does Gödel's incompleteness only 'mess up' with infinities or are there sensible finite systems
> that still are incomplete? ... Please provide short introductions to Presburger arithmetic"

Presburger arithmetic answers his question and answers Faizal's "definitely": infinite,
consistent, **complete**, decidable, because it has addition but not multiplication. Real closed
fields (Tarski's own 1951 decision procedure) are likewise complete and decidable and contain
elementary Euclidean geometry and much classical physics. A theory over the reals need not clear
the Goedel-Tarski threshold at all. Whether quantum gravity clears it is a genuine open question
with a plausible "yes" (discrete spin networks, integer-indexed spectra, microstate counting) and
it deserves an argument neither paper supplies.

## 2.3 The meta-theory is Tarski's metalanguage, renamed

This is the sharpest objection here, and the Lean file discharges it.

An **external** truth predicate always exists: for any semantics, the valuation itself is one.
`external_truth_predicate_exists` proves it with the term `⟨M.eval, fun _ => Iff.rfl⟩`, under no
hypotheses at all, not even the diagonal. Tarski forbids **internality**, a formula *of the
language* satisfying the T-schema *for that language*, and hands you the external one for free.
He says so in 1933: truth for $L$ is definable in a strictly stronger metalanguage.

P2's eq. (2) moves up exactly one rung of that hierarchy. Two consequences it does not draw:

1. **The move needs no Platonic realm.** ZFC proves $\mathrm{Con}(\mathrm{PA})$ and defines truth
   for arithmetic; Gentzen proved it in 1936 from PRA plus induction to $\varepsilon_0$. Those are
   stronger theories, not non-algorithmic understanding. Climbing the hierarchy is ordinary
   mathematics.
2. **Rung two has the same problem.** If $L_{QG} \cup \{T\}$ is still arithmetically expressive
   and closed under negation, then by the very theorem P2 invokes $M_{\text{ToE}}$ has no truth
   predicate for its *own* language. Either the regress continues, or you stop and call the top
   level non-algorithmic, which names the regress rather than terminating it. Condition (S4) says
   only that $\mathrm{Th}_T$ is not r.e., and $\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{N})$ satisfies that while being
   nobody's mysticism.

The pair `truth_is_external_not_internal` is the whole of the "Meta-Theory of Everything", proved
in nine lines, Platonic realm removed, nothing lost.

# 3. The established results, stated precisely

## 3.1 Undecidability of the spectral gap

Cubitt, Perez-Garcia, Wolf, **Nature 528** (2015) 207-211,
[arXiv:1502.04135](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04135). There is a family of Hamiltonians $H(n)$,
indexed by $n \in \mathbb{N}$, on an infinite 2D square lattice, translationally invariant with
nearest-neighbour interactions and matrix elements computable in $n$, whose ground-state structure
encodes whether a universal Turing machine halts on $n$. So $n \mapsto$ (gapped or gapless) is not
computable, and for some $n$ the answer is independent of ZFC. A 1D version followed (Bausch,
Cubitt, Lucia, Perez-Garcia, PRX 2020).

**What it does not forbid.** (i) Nothing about any *single* Hamiltonian: every fixed $H(n)$ is
gapped or is not, and undecidability is a property of the *function* over the family. Undecidable
never means "has no answer". (ii) It is a **thermodynamic-limit** statement; at any finite lattice
size the gap is computable by diagonalizing a finite matrix, and no laboratory holds an infinite
lattice. (iii) The Hamiltonians are **built to be Turing machines**, with large local dimension
and interactions designed to simulate computation. Not the Hubbard model, not anything a
condensed-matter physicist was going to write down.

P2's summary (*"deciding whether a given many-body system thermalises is itself algorithmically
undecidable"*) is fair; the conclusion drawn, that every route from Planck scale to smooth
spacetime "must contain steps that transcend algorithmic control", confuses "no uniform algorithm
over the family" with "no algorithm for the case at hand".

## 3.2 Uncomputable solutions of the wave equation

Pour-El and Richards, **Adv. Math. 39** (1981) 215-239; strengthened to "nowhere computable" with
Zhong (Math. Log. Quart. 1997). There is computable initial data for the 3D wave equation whose
unique solution is not computable.

**The caveat.** The counterexample lives in the sup-norm setting with data that is continuous but
not continuously differentiable. On $C^1$ data the propagator is computable, losing one
derivative, and it is computable on Sobolev spaces too. The non-computability is an artefact of a
function space in which the wave operator is unbounded, not a property of wave propagation as
physicists model it, and citing it as an impossibility for physics needs the extra premise that
physically realisable data can be merely continuous.

## 3.3 Chaitin, misstated

Chaitin: for any consistent effectively axiomatized $F$ there is a constant $c_F$ such that $F$
proves no statement **of the form** $K(x) > c_F$. The constant is about the length of a
description of $F$, so a few kilobytes.

P2 p. 4: *"any sentence $S$ with prefix-free Kolmogorov complexity $K(S) > K_{F_{QG}}$ is
undecidable in $F_{QG}$."*

**False as written.** Let $r$ be an incompressible string of $10^6$ bits. The sentence "$r = r$"
has Kolmogorov complexity above $K_{F_{QG}}$, because it contains $r$, and is provable in any
theory with equality. Chaitin restricts sentences asserting a complexity *lower bound*, not
arbitrary long or incompressible sentences. P2's next move, that *"ultra-complex statements,
inevitable in high-energy quantum gravity"* are "formally inaccessible", slides from the
complexity of the *physics* to the complexity of the *sentence describing it*. A short sentence
can name an incompressible object, as "$r$" just did.

## 3.4 The rest of the inventory

- **Wolpert**, *Physical limits of inference*, **Physica D 237** (2008) 1257-1281
  ([arXiv:0708.1362](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.1362)): observation, prediction, memory and
  control devices share one structure; no universe holds two that each predict the other, and at
  most one strong (universal) one. A genuine physics-flavoured impossibility, bought at the price
  of assuming a physical dynamics. [`omniscience.md`](omniscience.md) §2 row (d) places it
  correctly and warns it is proved nowhere in this repo; that warning stands.
- **Breuer**, Phil. Sci. **62**(2) (1995): no observer can distinguish all states of a system
  containing that observer.
- **Wolfram's computational irreducibility** is a thesis, not a theorem, and **Israeli and
  Goldenfeld**, PRL **92** (2004) 074105, is the sharp result, cutting the *other* way: several
  Class 3 cellular automata that are irreducible microscopically admit *coarse-grained*
  descriptions predicting their macroscopic behaviour. Irreducibility at one scale is compatible
  with predictability at another, which is the whole business of statistical mechanics.
- **Penrose's halting-problem argument**, on which both papers lean, needs the mathematician to be
  a *consistent* system whose consistency the mathematician can *know*, and Goedel II forbids
  exactly that (Putnam, Boolos, Feferman, Davis, Chalmers). The defensible residue is **Goedel's
  disjunction**: either the mind surpasses every finite machine, or there are absolutely
  undecidable Diophantine problems. A disjunction is not its first disjunct, and P1 and P2 both
  use it as if it were.

# 4. The public criticism, weighed

**Redden**, [arXiv:2512.11807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11807), charges that P2 conflates
**epistemic** incompleteness (limits on what can be proven) with **ontological** incompleteness
(limits on what can exist or be computed): undecidability constrains provability, not execution.
Conway's Game of Life is the exhibit, Turing complete, full of undecidable questions, and it runs.

**This is correct and decisive against P2's simulation argument.** P2 p. 5: *"Whenever an
experiment or exact model realises a property whose truth value provably eludes every recursive
procedure, that property functions as a concrete witness to the truth predicate $T(x)$ operating
within the fabric of the universe itself."* A physical system does not decide anything, it
evolves. Turning "this system's long-run behaviour is not computable from its description" into
"this system executes a non-algorithmic truth predicate" needs hypercomputation to be physically
real, a much stronger claim with no evidence behind it.

**Smith and Stocks** (2026-05-10) charge that the diagnosis is rigorous and the cure is
hand-waving: "non-algorithmic understanding" is never defined or operationalised, and is
unfalsifiable, since any failed algorithmic account can be declared non-algorithmic. Fair, but
weaker: unfalsifiability is a complaint about scientific status, not a demonstration of error, and
P2's authors could reply that $\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{N})$ is a well-defined non-r.e. object. That
reply survives Smith and Stocks and does not survive §2.3.

**Where the criticism is unfair.** The press framing ("physicists rule out the simulation
hypothesis") is not the papers' fault, though P2's closing *"our universe is definitely not a
simulation"* invites it. And dismissing the enterprise as mysticism misses §2.1: Hawking's 2002
lecture *Goedel and the End of Physics*, credited in P2's acknowledgments as the motivation,
reached a weaker conclusion from the weaker theorem, and granting Faizal the Tarski upgrade costs
nothing while making the remaining objections harder to wave away.

# 6. Three claims, three verdicts

## (a) No finite axiomatization predicts every phenomenon

**True, uninteresting, and not because of Goedel.** A theory of laws is not an oracle for facts.
Newtonian gravity is a complete consistent law and the three-body problem still has no closed
form; nobody concludes gravity is impossible. Predicting every phenomenon needs the laws *and* the
initial data *and* unbounded computation, and it fails on the second and third counts long before
logic is reached. Weinberg's *Dreams of a Final Theory*, P1's own reference [3], is explicit that
a final theory is one of principles, not a table of every event. Claim (a) is a straw man, and P1's
Axiom 2 (*"by definition should explain every physical phenomenon"*) builds it into the definition
of the object before knocking it down.

## (b) No consistent theory can prove its own consistency or define its own truth

**True, proved, the only one of the three the papers establish, and much smaller than the
headline.** Goedel II and Tarski, conditional on premises A, B, C of §1. What they forbid is
**self-validation**: a soundness certificate issued from inside. Physics never had one and never
wanted one; it has experiment, and it has metatheory. $\mathrm{Con}(\mathrm{PA})$ is provable in
ZFC and in PRA + $\varepsilon_0$-induction. A world formula that cannot prove its own consistency
is in the company of Peano arithmetic, which nobody calls impossible.

## (c) Some physically meaningful quantities are uncomputable

**True, well-supported (§3), and it does not entail the impossibility headline.** The reason is
the separation the Lean file exists to make. For **any** $f : \mathbb{N} \to \mathrm{Bool}$
whatsoever, computable or not, and any finite horizon $N$, a finite lookup table agrees with $f$
on $\{0, \ldots, N\}$:

$$\forall f\ \forall N\ \exists\, m,\, t : \mathrm{Fin}\,m \to \mathrm{Bool},\ \forall k \le N,\ \mathrm{table}(t)(k) = f(k) \veq{regime}\lean$$

So (`no_finite_test_separates`) no predicate whose verdict depends only on the first $N+1$ values
can tell $f$ apart from a finite table, the most boring computable object there is. Every
experiment ever performed is such a predicate. Uncomputability is a statement about an infinite
tail and no measurement reaches the tail. The contrapositive
(`separating_property_is_not_finitely_determined`) is the form to quote at anyone arguing from (c)
to impossibility: a property that really does separate an uncomputable $f$ from every finite table
is provably not determined by any finite regime, so whoever invokes it owes an argument that such
a property is physically meaningful. P2 never offers one.

## 6.1 The strongest honest position

*Concession:* if the laws of nature turn out to be a consistent formal theory interpreting
arithmetic, then that theory cannot define its own truth predicate or certify its own consistency,
and this holds even if its axioms are not r.e., which is where Tarski beats Goedel. Claim (b) is
granted in full.

*Demotion:* claim (b) limits **self-reference**, not **physics**. It leaves untouched every
prediction, every calculation, every experiment, and the possibility of a finite set of laws
unifying the four interactions. It has been known since 1933 and has never once obstructed a
physics calculation. Calling it "the impossibility of a world formula" rebrands a metamathematical
fact as a physical one.

*Residue worth keeping:* claim (c) is true and does kill one specific ambition, the **oracle**
reading of a ToE, in which the theory answers every well-posed question about every system in the
thermodynamic limit. Cubitt, Perez-Garcia and Wolf killed it in 2015 with a real theorem about
real Hamiltonians. That is sharper than anything in P1 or P2 and needs no Platonic realm.

**incompleteness theorems** are properties of *theories*. "Goedel-complete" is ambiguous between
them.

**This is the same error shape as the one under audit**, and that is this essay's organising
claim: a system is expected to escape incompleteness by a restriction that does not touch any
hypothesis the theorem actually uses. Removing choice does not remove arithmetic; being
anomaly-free does not confer deductive consistency. The Lean file states it as
`irrelevant_restriction_does_not_help`, whose triviality is the point. The productive question is
never "is physics incomplete" but **"does physics satisfy the three hypotheses"**, and that is
empirical about the eventual theory's language.

## 7.3 The escape hatch, and "Bloch Truth Mapping"

Every argument in this essay, and in P1 and P2, runs on a **two-valued** answer type. Lawvere
needs a fixed-point-free endomap on answers; Tarski needs a negation with $\neg p \ne p$. `Bool`
and classical `Prop` supply them.

Make the answers continuous and the engine stalls. If truth values live on a compact convex set,
the Bloch ball say, then by **Brouwer's fixed-point theorem** every continuous endomap has a fixed
point, so no continuous analogue of negation is fixed-point-free, so the diagonal yields no
contradiction. The liar stops being paradoxical and becomes a fixed point at truth value $1/2$,
the standard result in continuous-valued logic. Not a loophole this essay invented:
[`omniscience.md`](omniscience.md) follow-up lead 3 already names it, and it is the most
interesting unexplored branch in either essay. The owner's standing backburner project **"Bloch
Truth Mapping"** is, by its name, in exactly this territory. **Caveat, plainly: this essay has only
the project's name.** No document describing it was located; this is a conjecture about a title,
and `logic-bloch-poles.md` is where the material belongs.

Two deflations before anyone gets excited. Continuous truth values do not make omniscience
possible, because Lawvere's obstruction returns as soon as the answer type carries *any*
fixed-point-free endomap, and a discontinuous one usually exists. And a theory whose truth values
are Bloch-sphere points owes an account of what it means for a prediction to be $0.37$ true, which
quantum mechanics does not supply: Born probabilities are probabilities of definite outcomes, not
degrees of truth of propositions. That distinction is where such a programme lives or dies.
