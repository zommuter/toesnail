# 0. The headline, stated and not teased

**Six candidates were tested against four filters. Two survive with their structure derived rather
than stipulated, one survives by construction and therefore proves little, and the owner's own
first suggestion -- provability -- fails, for a reason worth having.**

The short answers, before the work:

1. **Provability as a modality fails, twice.** $\Box$ distributes over conjunction, conjunction is
   idempotent, and an idempotent element of a group is the identity, so no group-valued phase
   graded by $\Box$ is anything but constant. Semantically the mismatch is blunter: Goedel-Loeb
   logic is **complete for finite transitive irreflexive frames**, which contain **no cycles at
   all**, and a phase is nothing but a cycle. And the sibling essay already spends provability on
   the **$z$ axis**; asking it to be $\varphi$ too double-books it.
2. **Proof-relevance is the strongest candidate**, passing all four filters with the least
   stipulation. But it hands you $\mathbb{Z}$, the winding number, and $\mathbb{Z}$ is torsion-free
   while $U(1)$ is generated by its torsion. A $\mathbb{Z}$-valued invariant is **exactly blind to
   the roots of unity**, which are the only phases the actual gates ever write.
3. **Interference between derivations is real prior art, not an analogy.** Proof terms with sums
   and complex scalars exist (Arrighi and Dowek's `Lineal`; Díaz-Caro and Dowek's ongoing
   $\mathcal{L}^{S}$ programme, of which $\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{C}}$ is the complex instance). The price is that "proof" stops meaning "certificate".
4. **Gauge freedom makes a discriminating prediction, and it was confirmed by Feferman in 1960**,
   twenty-five years before anyone thought to ask this question.
5. **Three independent routes land on $\{\pm 1\}$, not on the circle** (the gates, the renumbering
   group's sign character, and proof-relevance's discreteness). That convergence is the most
   concrete recommendation here.
6. **The most-cited "phase" in logic is a false friend.** Girard's *phase semantics* of linear
   logic is a commutative monoid with a distinguished subset. No circle in it, and it is the first
   search hit.

# 1. Four filters, derived

The seed asks an open question, so the essay's spine has to be the filter, not the answers. Each
filter below is a property of the Bloch phase read off the physics, converted into a demand on any
logical quantity proposed to be it. A candidate that fails one is out, however well it reads.

## 1.1 Filter 1: the phase is never a property of one proposition

A global phase is unphysical: $\ket\psi$ and $e^{i\alpha}\ket\psi$ share a density matrix, a Bloch
vector, and every observable's statistics. The sibling
[`logic-bloch-poles.md`](logic-bloch-poles.md) uses exactly this to refute the prior AI turn's
proposal that `false` be encoded as $e^{i\pi}\ket 1$: that state *is* $\ket 1$, so the proposal
encodes nothing.

What survives is the **difference**. Given an assignment $f$ of a phase to each proposition, the
observable content is the two-place

$$ \mathrm{rel}(f)(p,q) \;=\; f(p)^{-1} f(q), \qquad
   \mathrm{rel}\big(c\cdot f\big) \;=\; \mathrm{rel}(f) \ \ \forall c \veq{gauge}\lean $$

and that is the whole of the invisibility: `rel_gauge` says a global left translation changes no
relative phase, and `gauge_rigidity` says nothing else is forgotten.

The converse is what makes "relative" a *structure* rather than a complaint. Any two-place $d$
obeying the chain rule is already $\mathrm{rel}$ of something:

$$ d(p,q)\,d(q,r) = d(p,r) \ \ \forall p,q,r
   \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; d(p,q) = f(p)^{-1}f(q) \ \text{ with } f(x) := d(p_0,x)
   \veq{chain}\lean $$

for any basepoint $p_0$. So "the phase is only ever relative" and "the phase is an absolute
assignment modulo one global constant" are the same statement. That is the definition of a gauge
freedom, and it is why candidate 4 below is not merely a metaphor.

**The demand on a candidate:** the quantity must be *two-place*, or one-place against a declared
reference. A property of a single proposition on its own is disqualified before any other test.

## 1.2 Filter 2: the phase is invisible to every truth measurement

The phase gates $S$, $T$, $R_z(\theta)$ leave $a_z$ fixed everywhere, so they change no
computational-basis probability $p = (1+a_z)/2$, and they act *transitively* on the equator. Those
two facts, usually reported separately, are together much stronger than "hard to measure":

$$ \big(\forall g,s:\ t(g\cdot s) = t(s)\big) \ \wedge\
   \big(\forall s,s':\ \exists g,\ g\cdot s = s'\big)
   \;\Longrightarrow\; t \text{ is constant} \veq{blind}\lean $$

An invariant measurement on a transitive action is not merely uninformative about the phase; it
takes **one value on the entire orbit**. The contrapositive is what the essay uses
(`not_transitive_of_separating`): if some truth measurement *does* separate two states, the phase
group is not transitive on them, and a candidate owes an account of what broke the transitivity.

**The demand:** the quantity must be *orthogonal to truth*. Knowing whether $P$ is true, in every
degree and to any number of copies, must tell you nothing about it. This eliminates "how true",
"how likely" and "how strongly supported" at a stroke.

## 1.3 Filter 3: a group, not an order -- and therefore not a join

This filter does the work, and it is where the predecessor's negative result turns productive.

The predecessor showed the circle has no invariant **order**. What it does have is a **group**:
$U(1)$ composes ($R_z(\theta_1)R_z(\theta_2) = R_z(\theta_1{+}\theta_2)$), has an identity and
inverses, and assigns an integer winding number to every closed path. A candidate must reproduce
that: composable, invertible, with a notion of going around and coming back.

Composability sounds easy, and it is where nearly every naive candidate dies, for one reason.
**Logical combination is almost always idempotent.** Conjunction is, disjunction is, joining two
bodies of support for one claim is, and Artemov's sum on proof terms is ($t + t$ proves exactly
what $t$ proves). In a group there are no idempotents but the identity:

$$ g\cdot g = g \;\Longrightarrow\; g = 1, \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
   f(a \sqcup b) = f(a)f(b) \ \wedge\ a \sqcup a = a
   \;\Longrightarrow\; f \equiv 1 \veq{idem}\lean $$

No commutativity, no associativity, no order, no finiteness is used: pointwise idempotence of the
combining operation alone collapses the phase to a constant. `phase_trivial_of_idempotent` is the
general form; `phase_trivial_of_sup` and `phase_trivial_of_inf` are the lattice specialisations.

**The demand:** the candidate's combining operation must be a *chaining* (path composition,
translation, transport), not a *joining* (accumulation of support). This is a genuinely
discriminating test and it is the reason the ranking in §8 comes out as it does.

For completeness, the survivor of §1.1 passes it: `rel_not_idempotent` says
$\mathrm{rel}(f)(p,q)$ squared is not itself unless it is trivial, precisely because chaining
$p \to q$ with $p \to q$ is not a legal composition. You have to chain $p \to q$ with $q \to r$:

$$ \mathrm{rel}(f)(p,q) \ne 1 \;\Longrightarrow\;
   \mathrm{rel}(f)(p,q)\cdot \mathrm{rel}(f)(p,q) \ne \mathrm{rel}(f)(p,q) \veq{survivor}\lean $$

## 1.4 Filter 4: the phase becomes visible through interference

Two paths that recombine add amplitudes. $HR_z(\theta)H$ turns an invisible phase into a genuine
probability $p = \cos^2(\theta/2)$, but only because the first $H$ pinned a reference and the
second recombined against it.

**The demand:** a candidate must say what "two paths recombining" *is*. This is the constructive
half of filter 1. A candidate that satisfies filter 1 but has no recombination operation has
explained why the phase is invisible without explaining why anyone would care that it exists.

## 1.5 The filter as a checklist

| | Filter 1: two-place | Filter 2: truth-orthogonal | Filter 3: group, not join | Filter 4: recombination |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Provability $\Box$ (GL)** | pass | **pass, strongly** | **FAIL** | fail |
| **2. Proof-relevance (MLTT/HoTT)** | pass | **pass, by construction** | pass, but gives $\mathbb{Z}$ | pass, weakly |
| **3. Interference of derivations** | pass | pass | pass, at a price | **pass, by design** |
| **4. Gauge: choice of formalisation** | pass | pass | pass, but the group is wrong | pass |
| **5. Holonomy over theories** | pass | pass | pass | pass -- all by construction |
| **6a. Girard phase semantics** | -- | -- | -- | false friend, see §7.1 |
| **6b. Quantum-cognition phase** | pass | fail | pass | pass, but fitted |

# 2. Candidate 1: provability as a modality

The owner named this one first, so it gets the fullest treatment. It deserves it: the *reason* it
fails is more informative than most of the passes.

## 2.1 What GL actually is

Goedel-Loeb logic **GL** is the normal modal logic $\mathbf{K}$ plus the Loeb axiom

$$ \Box(\Box A \to A) \to \Box A $$

which is the modal transcription of Loeb's theorem (1955): if $\mathrm{PA}$ proves
"$\mathrm{Prov}(\ulcorner A \urcorner) \to A$", then $\mathrm{PA}$ proves $A$. Setting $A$ to a
contradiction recovers Goedel's second incompleteness theorem as
$\Box \neg \Box \bot \to \Box\bot$.

Two completeness results fix its standing.

- **Segerberg (1971):** GL is Kripke-complete for the class of **finite transitive irreflexive
  trees** (equivalently, transitive converse-well-founded frames). Note what that excludes:
  reflexive points, and any cycle whatsoever.
- **Solovay (1976), arithmetical completeness:** $\mathrm{GL} \vdash A$ if and only if
  $\mathrm{PA} \vdash A^f$ for every arithmetical realisation $f$; the proof embeds a finite
  GL-countermodel into arithmetic via a self-referential "Solovay sentence". His second theorem
  identifies the logic of what is *true* about provability as $\mathrm{GL}$ plus $\Box A \to A$, a
  system not closed under necessitation.

So $\Box$ is not a decoration. It is the exact modal logic of $\mathrm{PA}$'s provability
predicate, with completeness proved in both directions.

## 2.2 Why it looked right: filter 2 is a genuine, strong pass

$\Box$ is **not truth-functional**, and this is the real structural match with the seed. The truth
value of $\Box A$ is not a function of the truth value of $A$:

$$ A_1 :\ 0 = 0 \quad (\text{true, and } \mathrm{PA}\vdash A_1) , \qquad
   A_2 :\ \mathrm{Con}(\mathrm{PA}) \quad (\text{true, and } \mathrm{PA}\nvdash A_2) $$

That is precisely filter 2's demand: a quantity that a truth measurement cannot recover. Of the six
candidates, this is the cleanest pass on filter 2 that is not true by definition, and the owner's
instinct that provability is "the other axis" is sound. It is just the *wrong* other axis.

Filter 1 also passes, and more interestingly than expected: provability is never absolute. It is
$\Box_T A$, relative to a theory $T$ -- and, as §5.2 develops, relative to a *presentation* of $T$
rather than to its theorem set.

## 2.3 Why it fails filter 3, structurally

Every normal modal logic satisfies the K axiom, whose algebraic content in the Lindenbaum algebra
is that $\Box$ distributes over meets: $\Box(A \wedge B) = \Box A \wedge \Box B$. And meet is
idempotent. So §1.3 applies directly:

$$ f(A \wedge B) = f(A)\,f(B) \ \wedge\ A \wedge A = A
   \;\Longrightarrow\; f(\Box A) = 1 \quad \text{for every } A \veq{box}\lean $$

The Lean statement (`box_phase_trivial`) carries the K-axiom hypothesis and **deliberately does not
use it**, which strengthens the finding: the collapse depends on no property of $\Box$ at all, only
on the phase being multiplicative over conjunction. So no refinement of the modality -- adding
Loeb, dropping it, changing the theory -- can repair it. The obstruction is upstream of $\Box$.

The second failure is blunter and is the one to remember. **A phase is a cycle. GL has no cycles.**
Its frames are transitive and irreflexive, and Loeb's axiom is exactly what forces converse
well-foundedness. Filter 3 asks for a count of times a path goes around; in a GL frame every path
terminates and none returns. That is the defining feature of each structure meeting the defining
feature of the other.

Filter 4 fails with it: $\Box$ has no inverse, there is no operation "un-prove", so two proof paths
cannot recombine destructively.

## 2.4 The bookkeeping objection: provability is already spent

Independently of all of the above, there is a reason the suggestion cannot work *in this project*.
[`logic-bloch-poles.md`](logic-bloch-poles.md) recommends, on counting grounds, that the poles be
`provable` and `not provable`. If that is accepted, provability is the **$z$ coordinate**, and it
cannot also be $\varphi$: the two are independent by construction ($R_z$ moves one and fixes the
other), so one quantity cannot be both without the ball degenerating. The honest form of the
owner's question is therefore not "can $\varphi$ be provability" but "**what is orthogonal to
provability in the way that provability is orthogonal to truth**". Candidates 2 to 5 all attempt
that.

**Verdict: candidate 1 fails.** Filters 1 and 2 pass, 3 fails structurally and 4 fails with it.
The failure is a result, not a gap: the phase is not any modality distributing over conjunction,
which is every normal modal logic there is.

# 3. Candidate 2: proof-relevance

## 3.1 The setup

In Martin-Loef type theory a proposition is a **type**, and a proof is an inhabitant. Types can
have many distinct inhabitants, and the theory keeps track of which. The operation that throws that
away is **propositional truncation** $\lVert A \rVert$, which collapses a type to at most one
inhabitant and recovers the bare truth value.

That is filter 2 passing *by construction*, and it fits better than it first appears. Truncation
**is** the truth measurement, and the information it destroys is by definition the information a
truth measurement cannot see. The seed asked for a quantity orthogonal to truth; type theory has a
named operator whose entire job is to delete exactly that quantity.

Filter 1 passes natively too. The relation between two proofs $u, v : A$ is the **identity type**
$u =_A v$, two-place in the syntax. There is no phase of a single proof, only paths between proofs.

## 3.2 Filter 3: a group, and a winding number, for free

Paths compose, invert, and have units at each point. That is a **groupoid**, not merely a group,
and it is the exact structure filter 3 asked for -- chaining, not joining. Iterating a path is not
idempotent, so §1.3's obstruction does not fire.

And the winding number is literally there. The higher inductive circle $S^1$ has

$$ \pi_1(S^1) \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z} $$

computer-checked in homotopy type theory by Licata and Shulman (2013), with the univalence axiom
entering through the universal cover, and with computational content: the proof converts a loop to
its winding number and back. So the candidate does not merely *resemble* a phase, it produces the
integer that counts turns.

## 3.3 The honest limit: $\mathbb{Z}$ is not $U(1)$

Here the candidate stops short, and the gap is easy to wave through.

$U(1)$ is compact, connected, divisible and **generated by its torsion**: the $n$-th roots of unity
are nonzero elements with $n \cdot g = 0$, and they are dense. $\mathbb{Z}$ is none of those, and in
particular is torsion-free, so a $\mathbb{Z}$-valued invariant cannot see a torsion element at all:

$$ n \cdot a = 0,\ n \ne 0,\ f : A \to \mathbb{Z} \text{ a homomorphism}
   \;\Longrightarrow\; f(a) = 0 \veq{winding}\lean $$

so no such $f$ is injective on any group with torsion (`no_injective_int_hom_of_torsion`).

**Why that gap bites here.** The sibling essay's §4.4 found that the phase the real gates actually
write is $\pm 1$: Toffoli with a $\ket-$ target imposes $(-1)^{a \wedge b}$, and
$\mathrm{CCZ} = \mathrm{diag}(1,\dots,1,-1)$. Those are order-2 torsion elements of $U(1)$, and a
winding-number invariant is blind to precisely them.

A second, softer objection. Identifying the groupoid of proofs with $U(1)$ would say any two proofs
of a proposition differ by a **continuum-valued** amount with no distinguished first proof. For a
recursively axiomatised theory the proofs of a sentence form a countable, effectively enumerable
set with a shortest member, and a countable set carries no free transitive $U(1)$ action. So the
identification is unavailable, and reading the phase as a *general* proof-relevance groupoid, of
which $U(1)$ is one very special object, is the defensible version.

**Verdict: strongest candidate, with a correction.** It passes all four filters with less
stipulation than any rival, and delivers a groupoid with an integer winding number rather than a
circle. The recommendation that falls out is to **stop asking for $U(1)$** and ask for the discrete
group the structure supplies, which converges with §5.3 and the sibling's §4.4.3 (see §8.3).

# 4. Candidate 3: interference between derivations

## 4.1 The obstacle, stated first

The reading is attractive: two proof paths to the same conclusion, constructive interference for
independent corroboration, destructive for conflict. It has one problem, and it is fatal in
classical logic. **There is no operation under which two proofs cancel.** Adding a second proof of
$A$ never weakens $A$. Formally, in Artemov's Logic of Proofs (1995, 2001), which makes proof terms
explicit as $t : A$ with operations $\cdot$ (application), $!$ (proof checking) and $+$ (sum), the
sum axiom is monotone:

$$ s : A \ \to\ (s + t) : A , \qquad t : A \ \to\ (s + t) : A $$

so $t + t$ proves exactly what $t$ proves. That is idempotence, and §1.3 fires:

$$ \mathrm{sum}(t,t) = t \ \wedge\ f(\mathrm{sum}(s,t)) = f(s)f(t)
   \;\Longrightarrow\; f \equiv 1 \veq{lpsum}\lean $$

So LP, the most developed explicit-proof logic there is, fails filter 3 for the same reason
candidate 1 does. This also disposes of the vaguer versions of the idea (evidence accumulation,
Bayesian confirmation, argumentation frameworks): all of them combine by a join.

## 4.2 Where it becomes real

Cancellation needs scalars, which means leaving classical logic for a resource-sensitive or
algebraic system. This is not speculation: it is an active research programme.

- **`Lineal`** (Arrighi and Dowek), an algebraic lambda-calculus in which
  $\alpha \cdot t + \beta \cdot r$ is itself a term, $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{C}$. With complex
  scalars, $t + (-1)\cdot t$ genuinely cancels.
- **Algebraic and vectorial lambda-calculi** (Vaux; Arrighi, Diaz-Caro, Valiron), where
  call-by-value against call-by-name controls whether superposition is preserved.
- **The $\mathcal{L}^{S}$ / $\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{C}}$ programme** (Díaz-Caro and Dowek):
  intuitionistic multiplicative-additive linear logic augmented with sums and scalars, with
  categorical semantics using biproducts, so that $\sum_i \alpha_i t_i$ is a proof of a
  superposition type. The calculus is $\mathcal{L}^{S}$ over a semiring $S$ of scalars;
  $\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{C}}$ is the $S = \mathbb{C}$ instance. Source paper: A. Díaz-Caro and
  G. Dowek, *A linear linear lambda-calculus*, Math. Struct. Comp. Sci. **34** (2024) 1103-1137.
  The overview cited here, *Towards a Computational Quantum Logic: An Overview of an Ongoing
  Research Program* (CiE 2025, LNCS 15764, 34-46; arXiv:2504.07609), is by **Díaz-Caro alone**.
  Its own subtitle is *"an overview of an ongoing research program"*, and that framing is what was
  verified, not any specific theorem in it.

So the answer to "what operation lets two derivations cancel" is: **linear logic with scalars, and
it exists.** Filter 4 passes by design -- interference is the semantics, not a metaphor for it.
Filters 1 and 3 pass: scalar multiplication is invertible and superposition is not idempotent
($t + t = 2t \ne t$ once scalars are present).

## 4.3 The price, stated plainly

You have redefined "proof". A proof was a *certificate*: something a checker verifies, and which
cannot be made worse by supplying more of it. In the algebraic calculi a proof is a **vector**, and
the monotonicity that made certificates useful is exactly what was dropped to buy cancellation. If
two derivations of $A$ can cancel, then possessing both is worse than possessing one, and it is not
clear what epistemic state that models. Quantum mechanics has an answer (the two paths were never
separately realised); a logic of proofs would need its own, and I have not found one.

**Verdict: real, with prior art, and second-ranked.** It passes every filter, and the reason it
does not rank first is that the pass is bought by changing what the object is, whereas
proof-relevance passes with the object left alone.

# 5. Candidate 4: the phase as a choice of formalisation

## 5.1 The reading

Global phase unobservable $\leftrightarrow$ the particular Goedel numbering is irrelevant. Relative
phase observable $\leftrightarrow$ the *relation between two formalisations of the same theory* is
not irrelevant. This is a gauge reading in the technical sense, and §1.1's `rel_of_chain` is
exactly its mathematical content: relative data plus a chain rule is the same thing as absolute
data modulo a global constant.

The obvious worry is that this is a restatement dressed as a theory. It is not, and the reason is a
sixty-year-old theorem.

## 5.2 It makes a prediction, and the prediction was confirmed in 1960

If the choice of formalisation were *entirely* unobservable, then any two presentations of the same
theory would behave identically. **They do not.** Feferman (*Arithmetization of metamathematics in
a general setting*, 1960) constructed a provability predicate for Peano Arithmetic built from a
different, extensionally correct enumeration of the *same* axiom set, for which $\mathrm{PA}$
proves its own consistency statement. Goedel's second theorem is not violated: the predicate fails
the Hilbert-Bernays-Loeb derivability conditions, and those conditions are precisely the statement
that the presentation is well behaved.

Read as gauge theory, that is a clean structure:

- the **global phase** is the choice of Goedel numbering, unobservable, because any effective
  bijection of the numbering leaves every theorem-hood fact intact;
- the **relative phase** is the relation between two presentations, and it *is* observable, because
  one of them proves $\mathrm{Con}$ and the other does not;
- the **derivability conditions** are the gauge-fixing condition, the analogue of demanding a flat
  connection.

That is a candidate that says something checkable, and the check has already been run, by someone
with no interest in Bloch spheres.

## 5.3 Filter 3: the group exists, and it is the wrong group

Renumberings compose and invert, so there is a group. Which one, and does it admit a phase?

A $U(1)$-valued phase on a group $G$ is a **character** $\chi : G \to U(1)$, and characters kill
commutators: $\chi$ factors through the abelianisation $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$. So the question "can the
gauge group carry a phase" is exactly "what is its abelianisation". Two clean cases:

- **Finitary permutations of $\omega$.** The abelianisation is $\mathbb{Z}/2$, generated by the
  sign. So the only nontrivial phase available is $\pm 1$ -- the two-element subgroup of $U(1)$,
  not the circle.
- **All permutations of $\omega$.** By Ore's theorem (1951) every element of an infinite symmetric
  group is a commutator, so the group is perfect and has **no nontrivial homomorphism to any
  abelian group at all**. There is no phase whatsoever.

I have *not* checked the abelianisation of the group of computable permutations, which is the one
this reading actually wants, and I flag that as an open sub-question rather than guessing.

**Verdict: third-ranked, and the most empirically grounded of the six.** Filters 1, 2 and 4 pass;
filter 3 passes as "a group" and fails as "$U(1)$", landing on $\{\pm 1\}$ or on nothing.

# 6. Candidate 5: holonomy over a family of theories

## 6.1 The idea

Deform a theory around a closed loop of interpretations and pick up a residual phase. This is the
most speculative candidate and the most interesting, and it passes all four filters --
which is the first thing to be suspicious of, because holonomy was *defined* to have those
properties. Parallel transport does not change the fibre's own content (filter 2), holonomy is
relative to a basepoint and a path (filter 1), it is group-valued with a genuine winding (filter
3), and the Aharonov-Bohm effect is interference of two paths (filter 4). Passing filters you were
built to pass is not evidence.

The real question is whether there is a **connection** on anything.

## 6.2 What prior art actually exists

More than I expected, and it is good.

Abramsky and Brandenburger's sheaf-theoretic framework treats a measurement scenario as a presheaf
of local sections and characterises non-locality and contextuality as **obstructions to a global
section**. Abramsky, Barbosa, Kishida, Lal and Mansfield then compute a **Cech cohomology** class:
in contextual scenarios the first cohomology $H^1$ of the presheaf of local outcomes is nonzero,
and the class vanishes when a global section exists (sufficient, not necessary, for contextuality).
Caru (2017) develops this further.

That is monodromy in everything but name: locally consistent, globally inconsistent, obstruction in
$H^1$. And $H^1$ with $U(1)$ coefficients is exactly where a phase would live. The logical analogue
is not far-fetched either: locally consistent but globally unsatisfiable is the ordinary shape of a
compactness failure, and Abramsky's logical Bell inequalities already put contextuality in
propositional form.

## 6.3 Can an independence result be read as monodromy? Probably not as stated

Here I have to be careful, because this is where the analogy is most tempting and most likely to
be wrong.

An independence result says $\mathrm{ZFC} \nvdash \mathrm{CH}$ and
$\mathrm{ZFC} \nvdash \neg\mathrm{CH}$: there are two models. That is a **disconnected fibre**, not
a nontrivial loop. Monodromy requires a *path* along which you return to your starting point with a
changed value, and independence on its own supplies no path.

Forcing does supply paths -- iterated forcing along a partial order is a genuine deformation -- and
there is a fact that makes the loop question non-trivial: ground-model definability (Laver, Woodin,
2007) says the ground model is definable in any set-forcing extension, so an extension "remembers"
where it came from. Whether that yields a nonzero invariant for a closed loop of extensions
returning to the ground model is **a conjecture I am stating, not a result I am reporting**, and I
found no prior art for it.

**Verdict: highest ceiling, lowest present evidence, fourth-ranked.** The cohomological machinery
exists and is quantum-foundational rather than logical; the transfer to a family of theories is
unbuilt.

# 7. Candidate 6: what else is out there

The owner invited "something entirely different", so this section reports a prior-art sweep rather
than an invention.

## 7.1 Girard's phase semantics is a false friend, and it will be the first hit

Anyone searching for "phase" and "logic" together lands on **phase semantics**, Girard's
completeness semantics for linear logic. A *phase space* there is a pair $(M, \bot)$ with $M$ a
commutative monoid and $\bot \subseteq M$; formulas denote closed subsets ("facts"); the powerset
is a commutative quantale. It is complete for provability in linear logic.

**There is no circle in it, no $U(1)$, and no relation to quantum phase.** The name is
independent coinage. Flagging this because it would cost an afternoon otherwise, and because a
literature search that mistakes it for prior art would report a rich existing theory of "logical
phase" that does not exist.

## 7.2 Quantum cognition: a phase that is already used epistemically, and its criticism

The one field that already reads a $U(1)$ phase as an epistemic quantity is quantum cognition.
Question-order effects and the conjunction and disjunction fallacies are modelled with an
interference term whose **phase $\theta$ is a free parameter** (Trueblood and Busemeyer 2011; Wang
et al. 2013; Khrennikov; Pothos and Busemeyer 2022). The Wang-Busemeyer model derives the order
effect from non-commuting projections, the Ozawa-Khrennikov model from a non-commuting
belief-update map instead.

**The good news:** the reading passes filters 1, 3 and 4, with a published quantitative match to
human data.

**The bad news, which is the lesson for this project:** the standing criticism is that $\theta$
cannot be determined before the experiment and is fitted afterwards, expanding the parameter space;
and there is a direct negative result (Boyer-Kassem, Duchene, Guerci, 2016) that quantum-like
models without order effects cannot reproduce the conjunction fallacy. The model therefore **fails
filter 2**: its phase is not orthogonal to the truth question, it is fitted *to* the probabilities.
A phase fitted to reproduce observed truth values is not a logical quantity, it is a free parameter
with a Greek letter, and that is the failure mode Bloch Truth has to avoid.

## 7.3 Two leads not pursued

- **Geometry of Interaction** (Girard, from 1989) interprets proofs as partial isometries on a
  Hilbert space and cut-elimination as an execution formula. If proofs are operators they have
  phases, and this is the one place in proof theory where a $U(1)$ could arise from the semantics
  rather than being posted in. I found and verified no specific result identifying such a phase
  with a logical quantity; this is the lead I would follow next.
- **Substructural logic of proofs**, where candidate 3's scalars and candidate 1's explicit proof
  terms would have to meet. It exists as a topic; whether the proof sum stays idempotent there is
  exactly the question, and I have not checked it.

# 8. Ranking

## 8.1 The criterion, stated before the ranking

**How much of the four-filter structure the candidate *derives* rather than *stipulates*, with a
penalty for redefining the objects and a bonus for making a prediction that can be checked against
existing literature.** A candidate that passes because it was constructed to pass scores low even
though it passes.

Other criteria would give other orders, and the owner may prefer one. Ranking by *usefulness to a
layered AI logic core* (the application his 2025-08-08 naming turn states) would probably promote
candidate 3, since a resource-sensitive proof calculus is implementable and a groupoid of proofs is
not. Ranking by *nearness to the physics* would promote candidate 5.

## 8.2 The ranking, as a recommendation

1. **Proof-relevance (§3).** Derives all four filters from a structure that already exists for
   independent reasons, and hands over a winding number. Correction required: the group is
   $\mathbb{Z}$ or a general groupoid, not $U(1)$.
2. **Interference between derivations (§4).** Real prior art with real scalars, filter 4 passing by
   design rather than by hope. Penalised for redefining "proof" as a vector.
3. **Gauge freedom, the choice of formalisation (§5).** The only candidate with a confirmed
   prediction (Feferman 1960). Penalised because its gauge group is perfect or has abelianisation
   $\mathbb{Z}/2$, so it does not carry the circle.
4. **Holonomy over a family of theories (§6).** Highest ceiling. Passes by construction, which is
   why it is fourth and not first, and the connection is unidentified.
5. **Quantum-cognition-style fitted phase (§7.2).** Works empirically, fails filter 2, and is the
   failure mode to avoid.
6. **Provability as a modality (§2).** Fails filter 3 structurally and double-books the $z$ axis.
   The owner's own first suggestion, and its failure is the most informative single result here.

## 8.3 The convergence worth acting on

Three independent routes in this batch arrive at the **two-element group $\{\pm 1\}$** rather than
at the circle:

- the gates write $\pm 1$ and nothing else -- $\mathrm{CCZ} = \mathrm{diag}(1,\dots,1,-1)$, and
  Toffoli with a $\ket-$ target imposes $(-1)^{a \wedge b}$ (sibling §4.4);
- the finitary renumbering group's only character is the sign, valued in $\{\pm 1\}$ (§5.3);
- proof-relevance supplies a discrete group, and $\mathbb{Z}$'s only finite quotients are cyclic
  (§3.3).

The predecessor essay already suggested this in one line ("not the whole circle, but the $\pm$
subgroup the real gates generate") and treated it as a loose end. Two further routes reaching it
independently makes it the single most concrete recommendation available: **the object to look for
is a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ grading, not a $U(1)$ phase.** A $\mathbb{Z}_2$ grading of a logic is a familiar
object -- parity, polarity, a positive/negative distinction of the kind focused proof search
already uses -- and it is far more likely to have logical content than a continuum of angles.

This is a recommendation. The owner may reasonably prefer to keep the full circle and pay for it,
in which case §5.3 says what must be produced: a group of formalisations with a nontrivial
$U(1)$ character.
