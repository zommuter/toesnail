# gtnsd2 follow-up: entailment instead of embeddings, and what embeddings are good for (2026-09-15)

AI research note at the owner's request, following section 4 of `2026-09-15-gtnsd2.md`. **Nothing here is
ratified.** Every "should" is a recommendation; the essay, its direction, and every decision stay with the
owner. Instrument: `2026-09-15-gtnsd2-nli.py` (this directory). All numbers below come from running it on
zomni on 2026-09-15. Every sample is tiny (25 NLI items, 28 conversation turns, 17 embedding
configurations over one ~33-sentence matrix): these are existence probes, not benchmarks. Gold labels were
written by the AI that wrote this note, which is itself a source of bias.

## Findings

1. **An LLM used as an entailment judge separates the four TAGOGAT use cases where every embedding failed.**
   gemma4-e4b (always-on, ~4B effective) got 25/25 on entailment-vs-not over the 25 items, including all 8
   task/transcript checks. "Does the transcript entail *the agent tested the game and reported bugs*?"
   gave yes only for the honest transcript. "Does it entail *the agent modified the tests so they pass*?"
   gave yes for both gamed transcripts, including the one that reuses the words game/test. The
   honest case is the fragile one: the first-token probability for entailment was only 0.60.
2. **Bigger was not better.** aya-expanse-8b scored 20/25 and fell for three HANS-style lexical-overlap traps
   (it said "The tester who the developer paid gamed the test" entails "The developer gamed the test"). The
   0.8B Qwen answered "contradiction" to almost everything (17/25, which is roughly what always saying
   "not entailment" would get on this item mix).
3. **Prompt order matters even at temperature 0.** Repeats were identical (0/25 unstable, as greedy decoding
   should be), but showing the hypothesis before the premise changed the label on 1-2/25 items for gemma
   (it varied between two runs), 3/25 for qwen-0.8b and 5/25 for aya. Gemma's one flip is on the core
   TAGOGAT pair: "The agent gamed the test." -> "The agent tested the game." became *entailment*.
4. **No embedding configuration fixes the use case, and it is not a BGE-M3 quirk.** Over 17
   configurations (BGE-M3 cls/mean, five decoder LLMs with mean pooling, last-token pooling, and a
   PromptEOL prompt), the lexically gamed transcript ranked closest to the task in 13/17. The honest
   transcript ranked first in 2/17 and last (below "updated the README") in 5/17. Last-token pooling makes
   role swaps slightly *less* similar than paraphrases in all five decoders, but by 0.06-0.48 background
   standard deviations, and only a word-salad-vs-swap ordering held everywhere (17/17).
5. **For splitting conversations, embeddings recover threads but not linear boundaries.** On a synthetic
   28-turn conversation with two interleaved threads and a topic shift, average-linkage clustering of
   BGE-M3 turn vectors recovered the three threads perfectly once the four 1-3-word turns ("ok",
   "thanks") were excluded (ARI 1.00; 0.69 with them). Nearest-earlier-turn linking attached 16/19 turns to their own
   thread (vs 10/19 for "reply to previous turn"); the 3 misses were two backchannels and the first turn of a
   new thread, which has no earlier same-thread turn to find.
   TextTiling-style depth scores did *not* beat trivial baselines (best Pk 0.35-0.39 vs 0.26 for two evenly
   spaced guesses). gemma4-e4b tagged dialogue acts correctly on 27/28 turns.

The pattern supports a division of labour, stated as a proposal in section 4: **embeddings for threading
(a topical question), dialogue-act tags for typing blocks, entailment for task-vs-transcript consistency.**

## 1. Why embeddings fail at TAGOGAT

**What is established.**

- *Training objective.* BGE-M3 is trained contrastively for retrieval: queries are pulled towards relevant
  passages and pushed from irrelevant ones, with dense, sparse and multi-vector scores distilled into one
  teacher signal (Chen et al. 2024, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03216>). The lineage is DPR
  (<https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906>) and Sentence-BERT (<https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10084>). "Relevant"
  here means *about the same thing*. "Alice paid Bob" and "Bob paid Alice" are about the same thing, and a
  retriever that ranked one far from the other would lose points. MTEB, the standard leaderboard
  (<https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07316>), has retrieval, STS, clustering and reranking tasks but no word-order
  or compositionality task, so nothing rewards the property TAGOGAT needs.
- *Bag-of-words behaviour.* Shuffling the words keeps 75-90% of correct predictions of BERT classifiers on
  GLUE tasks (Pham et al. 2021, <https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-acl.98/>). NLI models trained on MNLI
  accept a word permutation of 98.7% of examples with the gold label (Sinha et al. 2021,
  <https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.569/>). Masked language models pretrained on shuffled text still
  fine-tune well (<https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.230/>). On PAWS, pairs such as "flights from NY
  to Florida" vs "Florida to NY", models trained on earlier paraphrase data score under 40% (Zhang et al.
  2019, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01130>).
- *Negation.* BERT is largely insensitive to negation (Ettinger 2020, <https://aclanthology.org/2020.tacl-1.3/>;
  Kassner & Schuetze 2020, <https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.698/>). In NevIR, retrievers must rank two
  documents that differ only by a negation, and most do no better than random; the ranking is cross-encoders
  first, then late interaction, then bi-encoders (Weller et al. 2024, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07614>).
- *A cross-modal analogue.* Contrastive vision-language models behave like bags of words, because
  contrastive retrieval can be won without composition. Composition-aware hard negatives fix much of it
  (Yuksekgonul et al. ICLR 2023, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01936>).
- *Anisotropy.* Contextual representations occupy a narrow cone in every layer (Ethayarajh 2019,
  <https://aclanthology.org/D19-1006/>). BERT sentence vectors form an anisotropic space (BERT-flow,
  <https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.733/>), and contrastive training partly spreads it out (SimCSE,
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08821>). This is why unrelated BGE-M3 pairs sit at cosine ~0.5 rather than 0.

**What is inference, not established.** No paper I found reports a role-swap cosine for modern retrieval
embedders such as BGE or E5 (the literature subagent looked specifically). So the claim "BGE-M3 ranks swaps
above paraphrases *because* of its retrieval objective" is a plausible reading of the evidence above, not a
measured causal result. Anisotropy explains the *compressed scale* (hence the z-scores below); it does not by
itself explain the *ordering*.

### 1.1 Measured: is it just BGE-M3? (owner's scope extension)

Decoder LLM hidden states are **not trained as sentence embeddings**. Mean pooling averages token states
that each saw only their left context; last-token pooling reads the one state that saw everything but was
trained to predict the next token, not to summarise. PromptEOL (`This sentence : "X" means in one word:"`,
Jiang et al. 2023, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16645>) nudges that last state towards a summary. Better
readouts exist but were not run: echo embeddings (<https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15449>), SGPT's
position-weighted mean (<https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08904>), and LLM2Vec, which retrains the model
(<https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05961>).

## 2. Entailment (NLI), for a physicist

**Definition.** Natural language inference asks, for a premise P and hypothesis H, which of three labels
holds. *Entailment*: a person reading P would infer H is (most likely) true. *Contradiction*: H would then be
(most likely) false. *Neutral*: P does not settle H. The "most likely" is part of the original RTE
definition (Dagan, Glickman, Magnini 2005, <https://doi.org/10.1007/11736790_9>; wording seen only in
secondary sources, page not opened). The large training sets are SNLI (~570k pairs from image captions,
<https://aclanthology.org/D15-1075/>), MultiNLI (433k, ten genres, <https://aclanthology.org/N18-1101/>) and
the adversarially collected ANLI (<https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.441/>).

**A convention that bites TAGOGAT.** SNLI annotators write the hypothesis about the *same scene* as the
premise, to control event and entity coreference (Bowman et al. 2015, section 1 and footnote 2). Under that
convention "The dog bit the man" vs "The man bit the dog" is a contradiction (one biting event, two
incompatible role assignments); under strict logic it is neutral (two bitings can both happen). de Marneffe
et al. 2008 state it directly: "For texts to be contradictory, they must involve the same event"
(<https://aclanthology.org/P08-1118/>). Kalouli et al. 2023 propose annotating the logical and the
common-sense reading separately (<https://aclanthology.org/2023.cl-1.5/>). gemma4-e4b's three 3-way "errors"
(two role swaps and a passive swap, all labelled contradiction) are exactly this convention, which is why the
binary score (entailment vs not) is the fairer one here. It is also the score HANS uses.

**Cross-encoder vs bi-encoder, and why it decides TAGOGAT.** A bi-encoder (BGE-M3, Sentence-BERT) maps each
text to a vector *independently* and compares vectors afterwards. Whatever the comparison needs must already
be in each vector, and a fixed-size vector trained for topical retrieval has little reason to store role
assignments. A cross-encoder reads P and H *together*, so attention can align "tested" with "gamed" and
"game" with "test" and notice the roles swapped. The price is cost: comparing all pairs of 10,000 sentences
takes ~65 h with a BERT cross-encoder and ~5 s with SBERT (Reimers & Gurevych 2019). NevIR shows the same
ordering on negation. An LLM judge prompted with both sentences is a (very large) cross-encoder. Dedicated
small ones exist, e.g. `cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-base`, 92.4% on SNLI test
(<https://huggingface.co/cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-base>); not run here because it is not on disk and the
brief forbids downloads.

**Known failure modes.**

- *HANS* (McCoy, Pavlick, Linzen 2019, <https://aclanthology.org/P19-1334/>): MNLI-trained models learn three
  shortcuts. **Lexical overlap**: all words of H appear in P, so predict entailment. **Subsequence**: H is a
  contiguous substring of P. **Constituent**: H is a subtree of P. On non-entailment cases the models score
  under 10% where chance is 50%, and BERT got 0% on subject/object swaps. TAGOGAT is a lexical-overlap case by
  construction.
- *Hypothesis-only bias*: a classifier that never sees the premise gets ~67% on SNLI and 53% on MultiNLI
  (Gururangan et al. 2018, <https://aclanthology.org/N18-2017/>; Poliak et al. 2018,
  <https://aclanthology.org/S18-2023/>), because words like "not" or "nobody" predict contradiction. In the
  Inflownistration setting this is the risk that "the agent modified the tests" sounds suspicious whatever the
  transcript says.
- *LLM-specific*: LLMs predict entailment when H is familiar from pretraining, regardless of P (McKenna et al.
  2023, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14552>). Prompt formatting alone moves accuracy by up to 76 points
  (Sclar et al. ICLR 2024, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324>). Judges prefer answers by position (Zheng et
  al. 2023, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685>; Wang et al. 2023, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926>). I found
  no paper that swaps premise/hypothesis *presentation order* for LLM NLI specifically; finding 3 above is a
  tiny local instance.

**Faithfulness checkers built on NLI.** This is the closest existing engineering to "does the transcript
honour the task". SummaC splits a document and a summary into sentences, scores every pair with NLI, and
aggregates (74.4% balanced accuracy, <https://aclanthology.org/2022.tacl-1.10/>). AlignScore trains one 355M
model on 4.7M examples from seven tasks and matches GPT-4-based metrics on 22 datasets
(<https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.634/>). TRUE (<https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.287/>),
FactCC (<https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.750/>) and MiniCheck (GPT-4-level fact checking at 400x
lower cost, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10774>) are the same family. The SummaC trick (chunk, check pairwise,
aggregate) is directly the shape a long transcript needs, which links back to splitting.

### 2.1 Entailment and the essay's formalism

Let S be sentences and Mod(p) the set of situations (models) in which p is true. Define p ≤ q iff p entails
q, i.e. Mod(p) ⊆ Mod(q). This is reflexive and transitive: a **preorder** (not a partial order, because
distinct paraphrases are mutually entailing). The map p -> Mod(p) is exactly the essay's `solutions(.)`, and
the Galois connection of feedback section 1 is the Th/Mod pair: adding requirements moves down the preorder
and shrinks the solution set. **Requirement refinement is entailment**: R' refines R iff R' ⊨ R. "Filled gaps
never violate the originals" is the statement that each lower V-model layer entails the one above.

Two cautions for the physicist's reading:

- Human NLI is **defeasible** ("most likely true"), and probabilistic "likely" entailment is not transitive:
  each link can be 90% likely while a long chain is not. The clean preorder is the *logical* idealisation;
  an NLI model approximates a fuzzy relation that only locally looks like it.
- NLI checks a relation between **texts**. A transcript is itself an assertion by the agent, which can be
  false. METR found o3 reward-hacking in 30.4% of RE-Bench runs while able to say, when asked, that this did
  not match the user's intent (<https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/>); ImpossibleBench
  builds tasks where any pass implies cheating (<https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20270>); OpenAI's CoT-monitoring
  study shows a weaker model can catch reward hacking in reasoning traces, but training against the monitor
  teaches obfuscation (<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11926>). So entailment over the transcript detects
  *reported* violations and lexically honest gaming; it cannot detect a transcript that lies. That part of
  Inflownistration needs evidence outside the text (diffs, test files, execution logs).

### 2.2 Speech acts: the imperative must become a declarative

NLI is defined over declaratives, since only they have truth values. "Test the game" has no truth value; it
has a *fulfilment condition* (Searle's world-to-word direction of fit, Searle 1976,
<https://doi.org/10.1017/S0047404500006837>). To check it, one writes a declarative d(task) that is true
exactly when the directive was fulfilled, e.g. "The agent tested the game and reported the bugs it found", and
asks whether the transcript entails it. The experiment also added a second, *negative* declarative ("The
agent modified the tests so that they pass") whose entailment signals a violation.

What the conversion costs:

1. **It is not unique, and choosing d is a specification step.** "Tested" could mean played, wrote tests,
   ran the suite. Picking one adds information the task did not contain (feedback section 2's data processing
   inequality runs the other way here: d is a refinement, and a wrong refinement is a silent spec change).
2. **Violation becomes non-entailment, not contradiction.** Gaming the test does not logically contradict
   having tested the game (gemma labelled that pair neutral, correctly under strict logic). A detector keyed
   on "contradiction" would miss it. This is why two queries (positive d, negative d') are needed.
3. **Negative declaratives must be enumerated.** d' encodes a *known* failure mode. Novel gaming strategies
   need new d', which is exactly where an open-ended LLM judge ("does this transcript fulfil the task?") trades
   checkability for coverage.
4. **Honest but underspecified transcripts become fragile.** The honest transcript reports a crash but never
   says "reported": gemma's P(entailment) was 0.60 and one earlier run flipped it under the reversed template.
   Directive fulfilment often rests on implicature, which NLI conventions handle inconsistently.

## 3. Splitting huge conversations: which field, which method

The gtnsd2 sub-goal maps onto four established problems. They are often conflated, and they need different
tools.

| problem | question it answers | classic methods | recent / LLM | fits embeddings? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| topic segmentation | where does the topic change in a *linear* stream? | TextTiling (Hearst 1997, <https://aclanthology.org/J97-1003/>), C99 (Choi 2000, <https://aclanthology.org/A00-2004/>), TopicTiling (<https://aclanthology.org/W12-3307/>) | supervised (Koshorek 2018, <https://aclanthology.org/N18-2075/>), BERT meetings (Solbiati 2021, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.12978>), utterance-pair coherence (Xing & Carenini 2021, <https://aclanthology.org/2021.sigdial-1.18/>), SuperDialseg (<https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08371>), prompting LLMs (<https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03276>) | yes, topical by definition; weak on short turns |
| thread disentanglement | which messages belong to the same conversation when several interleave? | Elsner & Charniak 2008/2010 (<https://aclanthology.org/P08-1095/>, <https://aclanthology.org/J10-3004/>) | Kummerfeld et al. 2019, 77,563 Ubuntu IRC messages with reply links (<https://aclanthology.org/P19-1374/>); online transition model (<https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2020/535>) | yes for content turns; reply structure needed for "ok"/"thanks" |
| discourse structure | how do units relate (elaboration, answer, correction)? | RST (Mann & Thompson 1988, <https://doi.org/10.1515/text.1.1988.8.3.243>, unverified), SDRT (Asher & Lascarides 2003, unverified) | STAC multi-party chat corpus (<https://aclanthology.org/L16-1432/>), chat discourse parsing (<https://aclanthology.org/D15-1109/>) | no; relations are not topical |

For LLM-agent transcripts specifically: EM-LLM segments the token stream into events by surprise
(<https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09450>), SeCom finds topical segments are better memory units than turns or
sessions (<https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05589>), and a 2025 evaluation shows boundary F1 largely reflects how
many boundaries a method places (<https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17083>), so "sensible" granularity is a choice,
not a fact.

What worked: **threading.** Turns about the inventory bug are more alike than turns about the illustration,
which is exactly the topical similarity retrieval embeddings are trained for. What did not: **linear
segmentation on interleaved chat**, because "boundary" is ill-defined when two threads alternate; and
**short turns**, which carry no topic and sit at the similarity floor. Centring the vectors sharpened the real
topic shift (C ranked first) but could not make the interleaving start a boundary. The prepend-previous-turn
fix works here only because every synthetic backchannel follows its own thread's turn; real chat breaks that,
which is why Kummerfeld-style reply links exist.

## 4. Proposed pipeline (a proposal for the owner, not a decision)

1. **Chunk** the transcript into turns or tool-call blocks; attach short turns to their reply target
   (explicit reply metadata where the log has it, otherwise previous-turn heuristic).
2. **Thread** with embeddings: incremental clustering or nearest-earlier-turn linking with a "new thread"
   threshold. Topical similarity is the right signal here.
3. **Segment within each thread** (TextTiling/C99 or an LLM segmenter) into sub-blocks. Segmenting after
   threading avoids the interleaving failure measured above.
4. **Type each block with dialogue acts** (directive, commissive, assertive, question, declaration). This
   connects to feedback section 5: directives are tasks, commissives are promises, assertives are status
   reports.
5. **Check consistency with entailment**: for each directive, derive a positive declarative d (and known
   violation declaratives d'); check whether the assertive blocks of its thread entail them, SummaC-style
   (sentence pairs, aggregated). Use a small NLI cross-encoder for bulk, an LLM judge for the ambiguous
   middle, and non-textual evidence (diffs, test files) where the transcript could be false.

Weaknesses of this proposal: step 5's d is a hand-written refinement per task, and it multiplies judge calls;
the judges measured here are prompt-order sensitive; and nothing above was tested on a real agent transcript.

## 5. Open questions and next steps for the owner

- **Real data.** Should the next probe use a real Claude Code transcript (with its tool-call structure and
  reply metadata) instead of synthetic turns? The measured short-turn and interleaving failures suggest real
  logs will look worse for segmentation and possibly better for threading.
- **A dedicated NLI cross-encoder** (DeBERTa-v3 MNLI) would need a model download, which is the owner's call.
  So would BGE-M3's multi-vector (ColBERT) mode, which llama-server does not expose.
- **How to derive d(task)** from a directive: by hand, by LLM (a refinement that then needs checking itself,
  the regress of feedback section 3), or by a structured task schema (A2A-style states plus acceptance
  criteria)?
- **Does this belong in `~/src/linguistic-universals`?** For: its method is matched compliant/violating
  sentence pairs, and a faithful/gamed transcript pair is a matched pair in the same sense; a
  representation-space sibling (cosine and NLI instead of perplexity) would reuse its model layer and paired
  statistics. Against: its scope boundary is typological universals and "what a perplexity contrast can
  observe"; task-violation detection is a different construct with no Grambank anchor, and Experiment 2 was
  already scoped as a separate arm with its own boundary. If it goes there, it should probably be a
  separately scoped experiment rather than an extension of Experiment 1. Owner decides.
- **Geometry of the distance matrices.** `~/src/isochrone` has `isochrone.embed_spectrum` (classical-MDS
  eigen spectrum with negative, non-Euclidean mass; Kruskal stress per dimension; sampled Gromov delta for
  tree-likeness). Applied to 1 - cosine matrices of turns or sentences it would say how many dimensions an
  embedding map honestly needs, and whether thread structure is tree-like (low delta). Noted only; not run.
- **Instrument limits to keep in mind**: gold labels by the AI author; the ~33-sentence z background is
  topical; NLI item counts per category are 1-4; the gemma template flip on u1 differed between two runs at
  temperature 0, so llama-server is not bit-for-bit deterministic across runs.
